The problem

Every CT scan already contains 3D anatomy. Getting it out takes specialist software, a trained operator, and hours.

Locked in 2D

Clinicians scroll hundreds of axial slices and rebuild the spatial relationships mentally. The volume is there; the view is not.

Specialist tooling

Desktop suites demand installation, licensing and training, with manual format conversion at nearly every step.

Research code stops short

State-of-the-art models ship as scripts, not systems: no ingestion, no interface, no deployment path.

Land the middle card hardest — examiners relate to it. The third sets up the gap slide. Don't overclaim clinical need; this is accessibility and engineering.

Methodology — routing

The scan declares what it is. The system listens.

Every DICOM file carries a Modality tag. Reading it before any processing means one upload endpoint serves three fundamentally different physical measurements — no user choice, no guesswork.

CT

Calibrated density

Hounsfield Units are absolute, so tissues separate by threshold alone.

MR

Relative intensity

No absolute scale, and T1 inverts T2's ordering. Needs a statistical classifier.

PT

Metabolic activity

Measures uptake, not structure. Hotspots found by ratio against background.

One upload endpoint · three physically distinct pipelines · zero user configuration

The examinable point is physics, not code. CT is calibrated and MRI is not — that single fact is why one pipeline can't serve both.

Methodology — MRI brain

Without a calibrated scale, statistics replace thresholds

01

Series selection

Largest coherent series, sorted by slice position

02

Bias correction

N4 removes the scanner's slow intensity drift

03

Denoising

Non-local means, then normalisation

04

Skull stripping

BET, with a fallback for thin anisotropic volumes

05

Optional refinement

MedSAM sharpens the mask when a checkpoint is present

06

Tissue classification

Three-class Gaussian mixture, aware of T1 vs T2 ordering

07

Reconstruction

Marching cubes with Taubin smoothing per tissue

Measured output

Grey 1035, white 404, CSF 287 cm³

Stage 6 is where they'll probe. A GMM clusters voxel intensities into three distributions; because T1 and T2 invert the brightness ordering, the class-to-tissue mapping comes from the sequence type. Remember this one.

Interpretation

The gap is not a bug. It is physics.

Where thresholds win

Air inside the chest occupies an isolated density island, roughly −1000 to −500 HU, with nothing else nearby on the scale. A threshold is not an approximation of the right answer here — it is the right answer.

Learned priors add almost nothing, because the physics has already separated the classes.

Where they collapse

Bone spans a wide, overlapping range. Dense cortical bone is unmistakable, but trabecular bone sits amongst contrast-enhanced vessels and dense soft tissue.

No single cut-off separates them. Recovering the skeleton needs a model that knows what a vertebra looks like, not merely how dense it is.

Conclusion: route by structure, not by preference — the deployment and the anatomy decide the engine

This converts a number into understanding. Deliver cleanly: a threshold isn't an approximation for lungs, it's exactly correct; for bone no cut-off exists at all. "How would you improve the classical bone result?" — you can't with thresholding; it needs shape priors.

Repeatability is the floor of any accuracy claim: if the same scan gave different models, no error figure would mean anything. On the app's default path, the persistent GPU worker, nothing changed at all. In a fresh process or on the CPU up to 96 voxels at the organ's edge flip, which moves the surface by hundredths of a millimetre, a small fraction of the error we report. The meshing step itself is deterministic.

Limitations

What this project does not establish

The engine comparison is agreement

The classical-versus-deep-learning result scores one engine against the other, on one chest CT. The expert-labelled accuracy (slides 16–19) covers the deep-learning engine only.

Two organs validated

Accuracy against experts exists for the spleen and pancreas. The app marks every other structure "not validated" rather than guess.

No real-world test yet

No printed phantom in a real scanner, no other hospitals, no expert-agreement or surgeon study (slide 21).

Not a medical device

No regulatory clearance, no clinical validation. Educational and research use only.

Never apologise here. "Here is precisely what my result does not prove" — deliver all four flatly. Disclosing first turns a weakness into evidence of judgement. The first card matters most: the engine comparison is still agreement on one scan, while the expert validation covers the deep-learning engine on two organs.
